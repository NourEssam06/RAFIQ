# Safety requirements review

**Verdict:** The draft establishes controlled operation, an explicit unavailable observation state, local warnings, and approval gates for live email. Four acceptance gaps need resolution in the PRD or its named release protocols before supervised use can be accepted.

## High

1. **Verify the boundary between driver and supervisor interaction during motion.** UJ-1 covers controlled stationary use. FR-04 assigns manual resume to the driver, while NFR-06 assigns required interaction during motion to the approved supervised procedure. OQ-2 should explicitly define who can pause, resume, end, or cancel during motion and when the session must wait for the vehicle to stop. Require an acceptance scenario from warning or camera interruption through recovery or termination in the approved supervised setup. The scenario should demonstrate that the required interaction goes to the authorized person and that a driver action can wait until stationary.
2. **Set the fate of active warnings and pending escalation when observation is lost.** AC-06 suppresses new warnings and escalation from an unusable interval, but it does not verify what happens when a warning or an unattempted email is already pending. FR-09 says the condition must qualify again after a camera pause. Confirm whether interrupted dwell and cancellation windows reset, and give the user an unavailable status that supersedes any stale warning state. Test loss of observation immediately before send, restoration alone, resumed monitoring with a usable neutral feed, and a fresh qualifying condition. Keep the approved original sequence expiry in effect wherever the policy allows an attempt to remain pending.
3. **Define the dispatch boundary and terminal states for pending pilot email.** FR-09 and FR-10 cover recovery, cancellation, timeout, and confirmed sender acceptance. They leave materially different cases to inference, including an ended session, offline cancellation that races with reconnect, an in-flight attempt with an unknown outcome, and a later delivery failure after confirmed acceptance. Extend OQ-5 to define each case and test that the service independently rechecks current session and contact eligibility before every new attempt. Verify the outcome users see for a loss of connectivity before dispatch, an ambiguous outcome after dispatch, and a later bounce. Confirm which states can trigger a new attempt and how recipients are protected from duplicates. Preserve the distinction between service acceptance and recipient delivery.

## Medium

4. **Prove escalation and recovery rules beyond incident grouping.** FR-07 promises fixed severity, gap, recovery, cooldown, and repeat behavior. AC-07 tests overlaps, acknowledgment, short gaps, and incident closure, but omits when a warning must escalate, when repeated audio must remain suppressed, and how low-confidence interruptions affect those transitions. Add approved-policy fixtures for sustained qualification, repeated or overlapping detections, transient recovery, established recovery, and an unusable interval. Verify warning presentation under NFR-06 and evaluate false-warning counts and warning delay using the same policy definitions.
